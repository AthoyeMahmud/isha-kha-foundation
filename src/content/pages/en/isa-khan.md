## Isa Khan in Bengal’s history

Isa Khan was a sixteenth-century ruler associated with Sarail, Sonargaon and the leadership of the Bara-Bhuiyans. Banglapedia dates his life to **1529–1599**. His alliances and naval forces helped sustain resistance to Mughal expansion in eastern Bengal.

The reference describes a shift in his administrative centre from Sarail towards Sonargaon and places his death in 1599. These historical dates are separate from the dates of modern commemorative programmes.

## The Bara-Bhuiyans

The term refers to regional chiefs and landholders whose alliances developed during the transition from Afghan to Mughal power. Their composition changed between the reigns of Akbar and Jahangir. After Isa Khan, his son Musa Khan assumed leadership.

The familiar expression “twelve bhuiyans” therefore needs historical context. It describes a political formation, not twelve modern districts or a permanent membership list.

## A river setting

Bhati’s waterways mattered to the movement of forces and the defence of territory. Reading this history alongside the river landscape helps explain the importance of boats, channels and settlements.
